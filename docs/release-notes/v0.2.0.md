# LongClaw 0.2.0

LongClaw can now tell you that a newer LongClaw exists, and install it when you
say so. That is the whole release, and it is the first time the app makes a
network request at all — so most of what follows is about what that request is,
what it is not, and how to turn it off.

**[How to use it](../user-guide.md)** — project folders, the file format,
backups, agent setup, and recovery.

## Requirements

- macOS 13 or later.
- Apple Silicon. `LongClaw_0.2.0_aarch64.dmg` is an Apple Silicon build; there
  is no Intel or universal binary in this release.

## Updating from 0.1.0

0.1.0 has no updater, so this one is a download like the last one: open the
`.dmg` and drag LongClaw into Applications, replacing the copy that is there.
Your projects are files in folders you chose and the app does not hold them, so
there is nothing to migrate.

From 0.2.0 onward the app can offer the next version itself.

## The check

*Settings → Updates* is the whole surface. It names the version you are running,
says when it last looked, and has one button that looks now.

- **It fetches one file.** A static JSON manifest attached to LongClaw's own
  GitHub release, naming the latest version and where its archive is.
- **It sends nothing that describes you.** No account, no identifier, no machine
  fingerprint, no project names, no ticket contents, no usage. The request is a
  plain download of a public file; what a server can see is that some copy of
  LongClaw asked for it.
- **It downloads nothing until you press a button, and installs nothing until
  you press a second one.** Finding a version tells you about it. `Update`
  downloads and verifies; `Restart to update` installs. Neither happens on your
  behalf.
- **It runs once a day, and when the app opens.** Never in a loop, never with a
  retry, and never before the window is on screen.
- **It can be turned off**, in that same pane, and then nothing is ever
  requested — the preference is read before the request, not after it.
- **It refuses to restart while a save is in flight**, and says so. The refusal
  is enforced in Rust around the write seams, not only in the button.
- **A failure is quiet and stays in that pane.** A machine that has never
  checked and one that is up to date look the same everywhere else in the app:
  no badge, no toast, no dialog, no changed menu.

An update that is waiting is announced in two places and only two: a dot and an
`Update` link at the foot of the side panel, beside the version line, and a dot
on the `Updates` row in settings. Both carry their meaning in words as well as
colour.

The archive the app installs is signed with a key that is not the Apple one, and
every copy of LongClaw carries the public half; an archive that does not verify
against it is discarded rather than installed. The app is still signed and
notarized by Apple, and the archive is made from the stapled bundle, so a copy
that updates itself gets exactly what a fresh download gets.

## The local-only boundary

This release changes one sentence of it, and nothing else.

LongClaw works with no account and no sync. That is not a mode; it is what the
app is capable of. With no internet at all, every feature behaves exactly the
same — the only thing that does not happen is the update check.

- There is no sign-in, no cloud, no telemetry, and no crash reporting.
- Nothing about you, your projects or your tickets leaves your machine.
- The one request the app makes is the update check described above, to
  LongClaw's own GitHub release, and it can be turned off.
- The only thing stored outside your project is the list of folders you have
  opened, and now the update preference and the date of the last successful
  check, in `~/Library/Application Support/io.longclaw.desktop/`.
- Local diagnostics go to stdout when you launch from a terminal with
  `LONGCLAW_LOCAL_DIAGNOSTIC=1`, and are never transmitted.

Where 0.1.0's notes said *no network connection*, the honest line is now *no
information*. The boundary moved from what the app can connect to, to what it
can disclose — and the second is the one that was ever worth promising.

## What is not here

Unchanged from 0.1.0, and still separate phases rather than partial features.

- **Phase 2 — terminals.** Running commands inside the app is not in this
  release.
- **Phase 3 — sync and teams.** Multi-user projects, shared servers, accounts
  and billing are not in this release.

Also not in v0: Windows and Linux builds, custom themes, and hard ticket
deletion.

## Known limitations

Everything 0.1.0 listed still applies. Two are about this release:

- **The updater is macOS and Apple Silicon only**, because the release is.
- **There is no `Skip this version`.** An available update stays announced until
  it is installed or the check is turned off. A button that hides news is a
  button people press once and then stop being told anything.
