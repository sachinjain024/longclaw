Simplify the shell chrome

LC-239w · .longclaw/tickets/LC-239w/ticket.md

The shell says LongClaw twice and names the open project twice, and it spends the header's width on the first of those rather than on the filter field.

Read it in this order

  1. A — the lockup is simply gone. The sidebar now opens with Create project. Nothing else moved, so the header still carries the name and the field is still 180px. This is the floor: is it enough?
  2. B — the requested variation. Name, path, disk state and gear move to the top of the sidebar; the two create controls move to the footer; the header is controls only and the filter field takes the width. This is the recommendation.
  3. C — B with titleBarStyle: "Overlay". The owl sits right of the traffic lights and the native title is gone. Not recommended — see below.

Then walk projects 5 → 9 → 25, create Create project → folder chosen, press writing, and walk the three state buttons. Each answers a question the still frame does not.

Round 2 review, answered

  1. The block is one row of three parts. A 34px square tile carrying the project's initial, the name over the path beside it, and the gear on the right. 34px is the two rows: an 18px name line, 2px of gap, a 14px path line.
  2. Square, not round — a circle is this design language's shape for people (humans are circle avatars in the timeline and composer), and a project is not one. The tile wears the project's own accent at a 14% wash, which is the third place a theme reaches the sidebar after the row's dot and the menu's swatch.
  3. The gear sits on the name's line, out of the flow, rather than centred in the block. That is worth 34px to the row below it — six more characters of path — and the review's whole point this round is that the path be readable. It also puts a title's action beside its title, which is where this app puts one everywhere else.
  4. The path elides in the middle: <head>…<tail>. A tail ellipsis keeps ~/Developer/…, which is the same on every path in the app; the end is the folder that identifies the project, so that is the half kept whole. Walk path — fits · deep · /Volumes · brutal — against head/tail 8/15 · 11/12 · 14/9. Every one of the twelve combinations fits; the readout under the frame says so.
  5. 24 characters, measured rather than guessed. The chip is mono, so a character cap is a pixel cap. The box is 157px and a glyph is 6.32px. Two guesses were wrong first — 22 in a box I called 140 and measured 123, then 25 by a single pixel — which is why the readout prints fits or CLIPPED instead of leaving it to the eye.
  6. Hover gives the whole path. The chip keeps its title with the full absolute path — untruncated, not tilde-abbreviated — and the click still copies that same string. The elision is a display, never the value.
  7. The project ⋮ menu is drawn — press project ⋮ menu. It is ProjectMenu's real items in MenuList's real markup: Rename, the Theme submenu with its preset · appearance hint, the star named for what pressing it does, the danger row whose ellipsis promises the confirm, and All settings with its ⌘,. It hangs off the open project's row and is clamped to the window, as the real placement clamps to the viewport.
  8. The writing … slot moved below the tile-and-text row, full width. The tile then spans exactly the two rows the review names, and the list still does not move when a write starts.

Round 1 review, answered

  1. ✓ ticket.md is gone from the sidebar. Under a path chip it read as a second, quieter path rather than as news, and it sat there for seconds after every write. What is left is the half of WriteFeedback that is news — writing … while the disk is busy, silence otherwise. The header in variant A still shows it, because A is today and is what B is being compared against.
  2. The slot it used stays reserved — 13px, empty when quiet. Collapsing it would shove all 25 rows down and up again on every write. Press disk slot · collapsing with writing on and off to feel it; the reserved one costs one empty mono line and nothing else.
  3. The path is one line, always. The demo path is 46 characters on purpose. .path-chip caps at 180px because that is the header's number; in the block the cap is the panel, and the chip's own .txt already ellipsizes. Hover it — the title and the click-to-copy still carry the whole path.
  4. No hairline under the block. STARRED is a mono uppercase label with 12px of air above it — it already says a list starts here, and a rule in a 216px panel that is itself fenced by a hairline says it twice. Press hairline under the block to compare. The footer keeps its line for a reason this block does not have: the pair down there is pinned over a list that scrolls under it, and the line is what says the pair is not part of the scroll.
  5. Creating a project, from the footer — and what that turned out to cost. create draws both steps of the real CreateProjectForm: Create project opens it with no folder answered and the button naming the step that is next, Choose folder; folder chosen is where Open folder falls through when the folder is plain (LC-170), folder row on top, button finally saying Create project.
  6. The form is 523px tall and the panel has 560px of content at the 620px minHeight floor, so it does not fit under the pair. Two attempts failed in the drawing before this one did: a 46vh cap put the submit button below the fold of a nested scroller, and pinning the footer instead collapsed the list to nothing and still hung Create project 150px past the panel's bottom edge. So while the form is open it is the panel's body — the list goes, and so does the pair.
  7. The pair goes because otherwise Create project appears twice — the form's filled submit, and two rows below it the quieter toggle that opened it. That repetition exists today as well; putting the pair underneath is what reverses the emphasis and makes it a question about which one commits. The way out becomes a ghost beside the submit, which is the slot CreateProjectForm already renders when it is handed an onBack.
  8. At 780 — the height the window opens at — the whole form is on screen, unscrolled. At the 620 floor it scrolls about 120px to reach the actions. Press height 620 · min with create on to see the worst case the app allows.
  9. 25 projects. Press it with create · folder chosen on — this is the case that decides the ticket. The nav scrolls inside its own box, the footer stays pinned, and Create project is on screen at row 1 and at row 25 alike. Without overflow-y: auto on .project-nav this is exactly LC-73 again.

What I decided, and why

  1. The sidebar stays 240px. Narrowing it would re-tune section headers, project rows and the create form to buy width the header no longer needs — B already hands the field ~300px.
  2. The active row keeps its emphasis. The block above and the row below are not the same statement twice: the block is this workspace, with the path, the disk and the gear that only it has; the highlighted row is which of the five, which is positional. Press muted active row to see the alternative — the list loses its answer to "where am I" and the sidebar reads as five equal projects.
  3. The disk-state line goes to the sidebar, in a reserved slot, and says only writing …. It rides with the path, as screen-specs.md:64-73 says. The slot is 13px whether or not there is news: a slot that collapsed would shove the project list up and down a row on every write, which is LC-149's defect turned on its side. Press writing and watch that nothing below it moves.
  4. The gear moves with the path and stays outside the reachable guard — press unreachable: the name wears the warn triangle, the block stays, the gear stays, and Locate… is still one press away.
  5. The header does not render at all for an unreachable project. With identity and gear gone there is nothing left in it, and an empty 62px band with a hairline under it is a rule drawn across the top of the centred panel. Today's header keeps the identity row in that state; B has no identity row to keep.
  6. No project open → no block. There is nothing to name, so the sidebar opens with the sections and the main region draws Welcome, which is where the owl still lives at 52px.
  7. The filter field is flex: 1 1 240px, floor 120, cap 380. A number and a share: it asks for 240, grows into what the band has, and stops before it becomes a gap. Read the measured width under each frame.
  8. .project-nav gains overflow-y: auto. This is the load-bearing line in B. Press 25: LC-73 moved Create project up because the nav does not scroll, so at the foot of a long list the pair left the window. Pinned to .side-panel-footer over a nav that scrolls, it never does.

The owl beside the traffic lights — no

Variant C draws it, so the cost is on screen rather than in the abstract. A ~22px mark in the strip right of the lights buys nothing the app does not already have, and it is paid for with: a 34px inset on the sidebar that must collapse in full screen or the mark floats in an empty band; a data-tauri-drag-region that must not swallow clicks; hiddenTitle, which deletes the centred LongClaw that is the reason removing the lockup costs nothing; and a window-config change next to dragDropEnabled: false, which is LC-60's and would put probe:drag back on the critical path of a CSS ticket.

The mark cannot go inside the title bar. Every app that appears to do that is positioning webview content to the right of the lights, which is what C is doing here.

Recommendation: keep the decorated window. The name stays in the title bar, the mark stays on Welcome and in the Dock.

Open, for you

  • Which head/tail split — 8/15 shows most of the folder, 14/9 most of the route to it.
  • Is 14px/600 display the right weight for the project name at 240px, or should it sit at 13.5 with the section headers?
  • The tile is the project's accent at a wash. Should an unreachable project's tile go warn, as it does here, or stay neutral and leave the triangle to the row?
  • The 380px cap on the field — too tight on a 1600px window?
  • Below 900px the word Order disappears and the trigger stands alone. The alternative is the field at its 120px floor. Which?
  • Should the block's path chip keep click-to-copy, or is that a header affordance that a sidebar does not want?
  • With ✓ ticket.md gone, the app has no settled disk mark anywhere. Does it still owe the human a "that landed", or is the absence of writing … the answer?
  • The form's way out is a ghost beside its submit. Back is what the welcome flow's two-step calls it and this is one step — Cancel? And is hiding the project list for the duration of the form right, or should it stay visible and squeezed?
  • Moving the pair down is what forces all of the above. Worth asking once more whether it earns it, or whether the identity block simply goes in above a pair that stays where LC-73 put it.
